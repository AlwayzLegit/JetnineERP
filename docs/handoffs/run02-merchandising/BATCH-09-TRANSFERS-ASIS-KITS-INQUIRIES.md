## B. Wiring findings

### FINDING 117 — Transfers generate general ledger activity, and one report can print it
Invariant:  "The report consists of a **detail section**… a **recap section**… and, optionally, a **GL
            recap section that provides a list of the GL activity generated by the transfers**."
Option:     **`Print General Ledger Recap`** · `Include Activity for` · `Ship from Locations` ·
            `Receiving Locations` · `Location`
Evidence:   Report Merchandise Transfer Activity, /articles/15203028411668
Maps to:    **W-052 / W-053 — the first documented GL consequence anywhere in this section**

> Eight batches into Merchandising, this is **the first article to state that a merchandising
> transaction produces general ledger activity and to offer to show it.** Batch 2 established that
> landed add-on costs post to `General Ledger Assigned Account Settings`; this is the first *report*
> that exposes postings. Inter-location transfers move inventory value between locations, so they must
> post — but note what follows: **no purchasing article in the entire run offers a GL recap.** Receipts,
> PO closes, cost exception corrections and vendor returns all have GL consequences implied by run 1's
> posting table, and none of them is reportable from Merchandising. If we want the postings behind a
> receipt, we have to go to Accounting for them. Recorded as the strongest available evidence on
> `W-052`/`W-053` from this section, which remains: **Merchandising is almost entirely silent about
> what it posts.**

### FINDING 118 — As-Is is a reason-coded, dated, per-piece state with its own costing rules
Invariant:  "This report displays as-is goods in inventory along with each item's **warehouse and
            storage location**, **Reason code (that is, why it was moved to As-Is)**, **date it was
            added to As-Is inventory**."
Costing:    "**To include landed costs in the report, set the `Landed Cost Distribution` field in the
            `Costing Control Settings` to '`Allocate Upon Receipt`'.**"
            "**This process determines its costing method (either average or exact costing) based on
            the `General Ledger Postings` field in the `Costing Control Settings`.**"
Options:    `Reason Codes` · **`Include or Exclude Codes`** · `Inventory Type` ·
            **`Show Cost/Selling Price`** *(select `Unit Cost` for the costed version)* ·
            **`Piece Comments`**
Evidence:   Report As-Is Product Details, /articles/15202503193108
Maps to:    **NEW — and it resolves batch 2's terminology conflict**

> Batch 2 flagged `Landed Cost Distribution` vs `Landed Cost Allocation` as possibly two names for one
> field, with only the second article giving a value. **This article uses the name `Landed Cost
> Distribution` with the value `Allocate Upon Receipt`** — so they are the same field, the primary name
> is `Distribution`, and `Allocate Upon Receipt` is one of its values. One drift entry closed.
>
> The substantive finding is that **as-is inventory is per-piece, reason-coded and dated**, with piece
> comments — and its reported cost depends on two separate Costing Control Settings, one of which is
> the *GL posting method*. So **the cost of an as-is piece on a merchandising report is whatever the
> ledger is configured to post at.** That is a tighter coupling between costing configuration and
> operational reporting than anything found so far, and it means as-is valuation cannot be reasoned
> about without knowing the GL posting setting.

### FINDING 119 — Special order items are always costed at exact cost, whatever the settings say
Invariant:  "**For special order items, this report always returns the exact cost, regardless of the
            `Landed Cost Distribution` field in the `Costing Control Settings`.**"
Invariant:  "**If you have a previous period open and you back-date any transactions to that period,
            your inventory values for that period update to include the new transactions.**"
Piece statuses (verbatim): "**taken, reserved, unassigned, as-is, as-is assigned**" — with model,
            description, vendor, **serial/reference number**, current cost and reason.
Evidence:   Report Inventory Costs in a Storage Location, /articles/15202946429972
Maps to:    **NEW — and it completes batch 2's special-order costing thread**

> Batch 2 Finding 32 found that **special-order products have no average cost at all**. This is the
> other half: they are always valued at **exact cost**, and the landed cost setting does not apply to
> them. So special order runs on a completely separate costing model from stock — no average, no
> landed distribution, exact cost only. Given batch 3 showed special orders are also excluded from
> replenishment, batch 4 from distributed POs, and batch 8 from the slow-mover default, **special
> order is effectively a parallel product economy inside STORIS**, and a rebuild that treats it as a
> flag on a normal product will get costing, replenishment and reporting wrong at once.
>
> The back-dating note is the merchandising counterpart of batch 8 Finding 116: **inventory values for
> a closed-but-still-open period are restated when transactions are back-dated into it.** Run 1
> established fiscal periods and the End-of-Month; this says merchandising valuation follows them and
> is mutable while a period remains open.
>
> Five per-piece statuses appear here — `taken`, `reserved`, `unassigned`, `as-is`, `as-is assigned` —
> a piece-level state machine no earlier article named.

### FINDING 120 — Kit availability is the minimum of its components, and kits are valued as a whole
Invariant:  "**Maximum kit availability is based upon the quantity of the least available component.**"
Invariant:  "Quantity-on-hand and quantity available are displayed **for both the kit and the
            individual components. Net purchase order quantities for each component also appear.**"
Costed:     "displays the **unit cost for the kit as a whole and for each component**".
Evidence:   Report Kit Details, /articles/15202946027412
Maps to:    **NEW**

> **Kit availability is a min() across components** — the only derived-availability rule in the run
> that is not an arithmetic formula. It also means a kit has no independent quantity: it is a view over
> its components. That interacts badly with several earlier findings. Batch 3: kit-masters are
> **excluded from automatic replenishment**, so a kit going short depends on its components being
> replenished individually. Batch 4: **kits cannot go on distributed POs**. Batch 6: **transmitted kit
> components are locked until partial receipt**. Taken together, **kits are a derived construct that
> the purchasing machinery does not manage directly**, and their availability can collapse on a single
> component with nothing warning the buyer.

### FINDING 121 — Floor samples are a reason-coded as-is piece, and "unsampled" is a first-class merchandising question
Invariant:  "This report lists any merchandise that is **unsampled**, or (depending on the selection
            criteria) **not stocked at a specified store location by an as-is piece with a reason code
            of `Floor Sample`**."
Axes:       **`Stocked At` · `Displayed At` · `Programmed At`** · `Include Special Order` ·
            **`Floor Samples Only`** · **`Programmed Stock Only`** · `Warehouses Only` · `Stores Only`
Same-location rule: "If you specify the **same location at both the `Stocked At` and `Displayed At`
            fields**, the `Unsampled Stock Storage Location Selection` screen appears… you can compare
            the '**warehouse quantities**' of a location with its '**storefront quantities**'. To
            specify the same location at both… **you must remove the check at either or both of the
            `Warehouses Only` or `Stores Only` fields.**"
Constraint: "**To specify individual storage locations, the location must be location-tracked with
            storage locations already set up.**" · "If you specify multiple locations at one of the
            location fields… **you cannot run the report for storage locations** for that field."
Evidence:   Report Floor Sample Needs, /articles/15202930802452
Maps to:    **NEW — and it explains batch 8's `FLR` definition**

> Batch 8 Finding 108 found `FLR` defined by a reason code in `BMW_ACF`, with a fallback that counts
> every saleable piece at a Store location. **Here is the reason code itself: `Floor Sample`, on an
> as-is piece.** So a floor sample is not a product attribute — it is **a specific piece moved to as-is
> inventory with a particular reason code**, which is why batch 8's fallback had to sweep an entire
> location type when the code is unconfigured.
>
> `Programmed At` / `Programmed Stock Only` is a third display concept beside stocked and displayed,
> appearing here for the first time and nowhere defined. And the same-location comparison — warehouse
> quantity versus storefront quantity at one location — is only possible with **location tracking and
> storage locations configured**, so showroom planning depends on warehouse configuration.

### FINDING 122 — Inventory aging is per piece, in fixed 30/60/90/90+ buckets
Invariant:  "This report lists products and their associated costs **based on the age of the individual
            pieces** under **30, 60, 90, and 90+ days** columns."
Options:    `Aged Days` · **`Include Assigned Pieces`** · `Inventory Type` ·
            "If you select specific products at the `Product` field, **you inactivate the `Brand`
            field.**"
Evidence:   Report Inventory Aging by Product, /articles/15202930426004
Maps to:    **NEW**

> **Pieces carry an age**, so there is a per-piece receipt date underneath the FIFO layers batch 2
> found in the Costing Table. The buckets are **fixed at 30/60/90/90+** — not configurable, unlike run
> 1's AR aging which was parameterised. `Include Assigned Pieces` is the important toggle: **assigned
> stock is excluded by default**, so the headline aging number describes only unassigned inventory.
> For a mattress business where slow-moving floor stock is the main working-capital question, that
> default matters and is easy to miss.

### FINDING 123 — `View Merchandising Activity for a Product` is a DTS inquiry, and it is the section's index of everything
Invariant:  "**This routine is a STORIS standard DTS inquiry. You can modify its contents (via the
            `Dynamic Tab Settings` routine), but you cannot delete it. Since DTSs are user-defined and
            changeable, the descriptions in this topic may not match the DTS you see on your screen.
            This topic describes the DTS as it appears when you first install STORIS.**"
Standard tabs (verbatim): `General Info` · `Open Order` · `Serial#` · `As-Is` · `Special Order` ·
            **`Kardex Direct Ship`** · **`Kardex As-Is`** · **`Kardex Regular`** · **`Kardex Summary`**
Evidence:   View Merchandising Activity for a Product, /articles/15295212730900
Maps to:    **confirms run 1's DTS finding inside Merchandising**

> Run 1 established that **documented screens are shipped defaults, not specifications**, because
> Dynamic Tab Settings makes inquiry contents customer-defined. Here is the same warning printed
> verbatim in Merchandising, and it applies to the one screen that pulls the whole product picture
> together. **Whatever LA Mattress's version of this inquiry contains, the help center cannot tell us.**
>
> The find here is **`Kardex`** — four tabs, split Direct Ship / As-Is / Regular / Summary. A Kardex is
> a movement ledger, and this is the **first sighting of a per-product transaction history in the
> entire run**. Everything else has been positions and projections. Four separate Kardexes means the
> three material flows this run has been tracking — regular stock, as-is, direct ship — each keep
> their own movement history, which matches their separate treatment everywhere else. **These four
> screens are the most valuable unread articles remaining**, because they are where "what actually
> happened to this product" lives.

### FINDING 125 — Inventory reporting is pervasively cost-configuration-dependent, and three settings decide what a number means
Consolidated from this batch and batch 2:
| Report | Depends on | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Report As-Is Product Details | `Landed Cost Distribution` = `Allocate Upon Receipt` | Whether landed costs are included |
| Report As-Is Product Details | **`General Ledger Postings`** | Average vs exact costing |
| Report Inventory Costs in a Storage Location | `Landed Cost Distribution` | Ignored for special orders — always exact |
| Report Beginning of Month… (b8) | two open accounting periods | "Beginning of month" means the earlier one |
| Report Inventory Costs in a Storage Location | back-dated transactions into an open period | **Values for that period are restated** |
| GMROI / Turns / GM12 / GM4 (b8) | fixed rule | Exclude freight and add-ons 1 and 2 |
| Report Product Performance `R`/`A` (b8) | fixed rule | **Include** the freight factor |
| Report Comparative Kits | fixed rule | Uses **weighted average cost** specifically |
Evidence:   accumulated; Report As-Is Product Details, /articles/15202503193108; Report Inventory Costs
            in a Storage Location, /articles/15202946429972; Report Comparative Kits,
            /articles/15202503206804
Maps to:    **NEW — recorded as a finding because no article states it**

> **There is no single inventory cost in STORIS reporting.** Eight reports in this run use at least
> five different cost bases, three of them selected by configuration and two hardcoded per report, and
> **only some of the articles say which.** `Report Comparative Kits` names weighted average cost
> explicitly; `Report As-Is Product Details` defers to the GL posting setting; the KPIs strip freight;
> the margin columns add it back.
>
> For the parity work this is the practical warning: **any number we reconcile against STORIS during
> cutover must be matched to the exact report that produced it**, because two reports of "inventory
> value at cost" can legitimately differ and neither is wrong. This belongs in the run summary.

### FINDING 126 — Product classification is a five-level hierarchy plus two cross-cutting axes
Consolidated (verbatim field names across the run):
            **Category** → **Group** *(`View Product Category Details`: "identify the product **groups**
            that belong to a specific product **category**")* — so **category contains groups**.
            **Collection** — carries the **price point** (`PP`, batch 8: "the price point as it is
            defined in this item's **primary collection record**") and a **cut date**.
            **Open to Buy (OTB) Department** — an independent axis used for buying and reporting.
            **Brand** — an independent axis, mutually exclusive with Product in aging selection.
            **Style** — a product-record code (batch 5, batch 8), never defined.
            **Vendor / Vendor Ship-From** and **Buying Group** — supply-side axes.
Evidence:   View Product Category Details, /articles/15295210641300; Report Product Performance,
            /articles/15203112358932; accumulated across batches 5, 8, 9
Maps to:    **NEW — assembled; no single article states the hierarchy**

> Nine batches in, the product classification model can finally be stated, and **no article anywhere
> lays it out**. Category contains groups; collection is separate and owns the price point and cut
> date; open-to-buy department, brand and style are further independent axes. **Price point is a
> property of the collection, not the product** — so repricing a collection moves a merchandising
> attribute of every product in it, which nothing in the merchandising-decisions wizard (batch 5
> Finding 71) mentions. And **cut date lives on the vendor subsidiary record for the collection**
> (batch 8), so the same collection can have different cut dates by vendor. Any rebuild needs this
> shape settled early: it is the spine of every report in batches 5, 8 and 9.

## G. Sequencing rules

1. As-is reporting includes landed costs only when `Landed Cost Distribution` is `Allocate Upon
   Receipt`; its average-vs-exact basis follows `General Ledger Postings`.
2. Special order items are always reported at exact cost regardless of that setting.
3. Back-dating a transaction into an open prior period **restates that period's inventory values**.
4. Kit availability is capped by the least available component.
5. Storage-location floor-sample comparison requires location tracking to be configured.
6. Specifying multiple locations in one location field disables storage-location reporting for it.
7. Selecting specific products disables the Brand selection on the aging report.
8. Assigned pieces are excluded from aging unless `Include Assigned Pieces` is set.
